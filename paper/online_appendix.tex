\section{Service-Level--Recensoring Grid Results}\label{oa:grid}

Tables~\ref{tab:full_conversion} and~\ref{tab:full_mechanism} report the complete results for the $5\times 5$ service-level--recensoring grid experiment.
Operationally, $\lambda$ is the share of identified stockout-month records evaluated as sales-only rather than demand-recovered observations; the five grid values correspond to scored-origin recensoring rates of 0\%, 9.1\%, 18.3\%, 27.3\%, and 36.5\%.
The conversion family contains 50 estimands ($25\;R_0+25\;R_3$) with simultaneous critical value $c_{R_0,R_3}=2.763$.
The mechanism family contains 45 estimands ($25\;A+20$ non-mechanical $\mathrm{DiD}$) with $c_{A,\mathrm{DiD}}=2.895$.
All intervals use $B=50{,}000$ two-way bootstrap resamples over SKU clusters and latent-demand draws with draw-specific SHA-256 recensoring orderings.
For coordinate $j$, let $\widehat\theta_j^{*(b)}$ denote its estimate in bootstrap replication $b$ and let $\widehat s_j=\operatorname{sd}_b(\widehat\theta_j^{*(b)})$.
The replication statistic is
\[
T_b=\max_{j\in\mathcal J}
\left|\frac{\widehat\theta_j^{*(b)}-\widehat\theta_j}{\widehat s_j}\right|,
\]
and the 95th percentile of $T_b$ supplies the family critical value.
Each attenuation and DiD is formed within the replication before $\widehat s_j$ is computed, thereby retaining all component covariance; the resulting bands control familywise error at 5\% within each prespecified family.
Throughout this section, $R_0$ and $R_3$ are relative cost effects; negative values indicate lower cost for Chronos-2 than for the retuned empirical baseline.

\subsection{\add{Promised Lead-Time Replay}}\label{oa:promised-lead-time}

\add{The main Chinese-supermarket replay assigns every new order a fixed one-day lead time. To assess that assumption without using future information, we hold every forecast-induced stock target fixed and replace only the arrival schedule. At each review day $t$, we draw the lead time for SKU $i$ from the empirical distribution of \texttt{normal\_lead\_time} among its orders placed strictly before $t$:}
\[
\add{L_{it}\sim\widehat F_{i,t}^{\mathrm{promise}},
\qquad
\widehat F_{i,t}^{\mathrm{promise}}
=\frac{1}{|\mathcal H_{i,t}|}
\sum_{o\in\mathcal H_{i,t}}\delta_{\ell_o},
\quad
\mathcal H_{i,t}=\{o:\mathrm{SKU}_o=i,\ \mathrm{order\_date}_o<t\}.}
\]
\add{All 1{,}135 SKUs have nonempty own-SKU histories at each of the 13 review dates, so no pooled fallback is used. The source's 0-day promised lead times are mapped to one day because the replay receives arrivals before placing new orders; this is the shortest feasible arrival under the model's daily sequence. The actual \texttt{arrival\_date} field is never used. Thus, this check represents heterogeneity in promised lead times, not uncertainty in delivery relative to a promise.}

\add{We generate 50 schedules using SHA-256 seeds keyed by SKU and review date and apply the same schedule to every policy arm within a draw. Across review dates, the mean effective lead time is 1.78 days, its 90th percentile is four days, and the largest sampled value is eight days. Confidence intervals jointly resample the 1{,}135 SKU clusters and the 50 lead-time schedules.}

\subsection{Latent-Demand Generator Sensitivity}\label{oa:dgp}

The baseline empirical-conditional generator classifies a series--origin month as censored when monthly logged sales weakly exceed positive beginning inventory.
For each series, it forms a donor distribution from daily sales in origin months not so classified and, on each positive-sales day in a classified month, draws from that empirical distribution conditional on the draw being at least the logged sale.
An empty conditional donor set invokes the geometric-excess fallback described in the main paper.

Two parametric generators use the same classified months and imputed days.
Let $\widehat\mu_i$ and $\widehat v_i$ be the mean and sample variance of series $i$'s daily sales in nonclassified origin months.
The Poisson generator draws
\[
X_{it}\sim \operatorname{Poisson}(\widehat\mu_i)\mid X_{it}\geq Y_{it}.
\]
When $\widehat v_i>\widehat\mu_i$, the negative-binomial generator uses
\[
r_i=\frac{\widehat\mu_i^2}{\widehat v_i-\widehat\mu_i},
\qquad
q_i=\frac{r_i}{r_i+\widehat\mu_i},
\qquad
X_{it}\sim \operatorname{NB}(r_i,q_i)\mid X_{it}\geq Y_{it};
\]
otherwise it uses the Poisson specification.
Numerically degenerate truncations use the same series-specific geometric fallback.
All three generators leave every other day equal to logged sales and use 50 SHA-256-keyed draws.
This construction preserves the observed zero mass and timing outside the common set of positive-sales imputation cells.

Table~\ref{tab:oa_dgp_diag} summarizes positive imputed increments, $D-Y>0$.
The three specifications span a transparent severity range: Poisson has the lightest positive excess, the moment-matched negative binomial is intermediate, and empirical conditional resampling has the largest mean and upper tail.

\subsection{PMF Reconstruction and Protection-Interval Dependence}\label{oa:aggregation}

The baseline PMF maps the repaired 21-point quantile grid to the integer support by taking the midpoint of the lower and upper CDF bounds implied at each support point.
The first reconstruction check instead linearly interpolates the repaired inverse quantile function between adjacent quantile levels and numerically inverts it on the integer support.
The second retains midpoint reconstruction but uses the 12 levels
\[
\{0.01,0.10,0.20,0.30,0.40,0.50,0.60,0.70,0.80,0.90,0.95,0.99\}.
\]

The dependence checks preserve these daily marginal CDFs and generate protection-interval demand through a Gaussian-copula AR(1) design.
For independent scrambled Sobol normal scores $\epsilon_t$, we set
\[
Z_1=\epsilon_1,
\qquad
Z_t=\rho Z_{t-1}+\sqrt{1-\rho^2}\,\epsilon_t,
\qquad
U_t=\Phi(Z_t),
\]
and obtain daily integer demand by applying each forecast CDF's inverse to $U_t$.
The stocking target is the empirical $\alpha$ quantile of 16{,}384 simulated protection-interval sums.
The stress values $\rho=0.25$ and $0.50$ are applied identically to every arm.

\section{\add{Proofs and Known-Truth Validation of Policy-Dependent Censoring Bias}}\label{oa:known-truth}

\subsection{\add{Proof of Proposition 1 and Static Extensions}}

\proof{\add{Proof of Proposition 1.}}
\add{Fix a cell $j$ and write $\delta_j=S_j^H-S_j^L\geq0$ and $g_j(x)=c_j(S_j^H,x)-c_j(S_j^L,x)$. Clamp $x$ to the interval between the targets:}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:oa-clamp}
\add{\Pi_j(x)=\min\{S_j^H,\max\{S_j^L,x\}\}.}
\end{equation}
\add{If $x\leq S_j^L$, direct substitution gives $g_j(x)=h_j\delta_j$; if $S_j^L<x<S_j^H$, it gives $g_j(x)=h_j(S_j^H-x)-p_j(x-S_j^L)$; and if $x\geq S_j^H$, it gives $g_j(x)=-p_j\delta_j$. The three cases, including their common boundary values, are equivalently}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:oa-gap-clamp}
\add{g_j(x)=h_j\delta_j-(h_j+p_j)\bigl[\Pi_j(x)-S_j^L\bigr].}
\end{equation}
\add{Because $Y_j\leq D_j$, the difference $\Pi_j(D_j)-\Pi_j(Y_j)$ is the length of the intersection $(Y_j,D_j]\cap(S_j^L,S_j^H]$. Hence}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:oa-clamp-difference}
\add{\Pi_j(D_j)-\Pi_j(Y_j)=\left[\min\{D_j,S_j^H\}-\max\{Y_j,S_j^L\}\right]^+.}
\end{equation}
\add{Subtracting Equation~\eqref{eq:oa-gap-clamp} at $D_j$ from the same equation at $Y_j$ and applying Equation~\eqref{eq:oa-clamp-difference} yields}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:oa-static-proof}
\add{g_j(Y_j)-g_j(D_j)=(h_j+p_j)\left[\min\{D_j,S_j^H\}-\max\{Y_j,S_j^L\}\right]^+.}
\end{equation}
\add{Summing Equation~\eqref{eq:oa-static-proof} over cells proves the stated identity and $W^{\mathrm{st}}\geq0$. Since $h_j+p_j>0$, the sum is strictly positive exactly when $(Y_j,D_j]$ and $(S_j^L,S_j^H]$ have a positive-length intersection for at least one cell. Finally, $\Delta_Y^{\mathrm{st}}=\Delta_D^{\mathrm{st}}+W^{\mathrm{st}}$. Conditional on $\Delta_D^{\mathrm{st}}<0$, the logged-sales evaluation selects $L$ rather than $H$ exactly when $\Delta_Y^{\mathrm{st}}>0$, or equivalently $W^{\mathrm{st}}> -\Delta_D^{\mathrm{st}}$; equality gives a tie. This proves every claim in Proposition~1. \qed}
\endproof

\paragraph{\add{Equivalent tail-probability condition.}}
\add{With common targets $S^H>S^L$, common costs, $\delta=S^H-S^L$, critical ratio $\alpha=p/(h+p)$, and $\Pi(x)=\min\{S^H,\max\{S^L,x\}\}$, the layer-cake identity gives}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:oa-layer-cake}
\add{\Pi(X)-S^L=\int_{S^L}^{S^H}\mathbf 1\{X>u\}\,du.}
\end{equation}
\add{Define the average tail probability between the targets as}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:oa-average-tail}
\add{\bar q_X=\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{S^L}^{S^H}\Pr(X>u)\,du.}
\end{equation}
\add{Taking expectations in Equations~\eqref{eq:oa-gap-clamp} and~\eqref{eq:oa-layer-cake}, and interchanging expectation and the bounded integral, yields}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:oa-tail-condition}
\add{\mathbb E[g(X)]=(h+p)\delta\bigl[(1-\alpha)-\bar q_X\bigr].}
\end{equation}
\add{Therefore $H$ is preferred under $D$ and $L$ under $Y$ exactly when $\bar q_Y<1-\alpha<\bar q_D$, with equality on either side producing a tie. This equivalent condition clarifies why the overall censoring rate is insufficient: only censored probability mass crossing thresholds between the targets changes their comparison.}

\paragraph{\add{Targets that cross across cells.}}
\add{If two policies' targets cross across cells, let $S_j^+=\max\{S_{1j},S_{0j}\}$, $S_j^-=\min\{S_{1j},S_{0j}\}$, and $\sigma_j=\operatorname{sgn}(S_{1j}-S_{0j})$. The exact signed extension is}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:oa-signed-overlap}
\add{W_{1-0}^{\mathrm{st}}=\sum_j\sigma_j(h_j+p_j)\left[\min\{D_j,S_j^+\}-\max\{Y_j,S_j^-\}\right]^+.}
\end{equation}
\add{To verify Equation~\eqref{eq:oa-signed-overlap}, apply Proposition~1 cellwise when $\sigma_j=1$, interchange the two policies when $\sigma_j=-1$, and note that both sides vanish when $\sigma_j=0$. Consequently, an average target ordering alone does not determine the bias direction; the cell-level signed overlaps must be computed.}

\subsection{\add{Proof of Proposition 2}}

\proof{\add{Proof of Proposition 2.}}
\add{For each fixed policy $a$, total replay cost satisfies $C_a(X)=\mathcal H_a(X)+\mathcal P_a(X)$. Expanding $W^{\mathrm{CL}}=\Delta_Y^{\mathrm{CL}}-\Delta_D^{\mathrm{CL}}$ and collecting policy-specific changes gives}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:oa-closed-loop-proof}
\begin{aligned}
\add{W^{\mathrm{CL}}}
&\add{=\bigl[\mathcal H_H(Y)-\mathcal H_H(D)\bigr]-\bigl[\mathcal H_L(Y)-\mathcal H_L(D)\bigr]}\\
&\quad\add{+\bigl[\mathcal P_H(Y)-\mathcal P_H(D)\bigr]-\bigl[\mathcal P_L(Y)-\mathcal P_L(D)\bigr]}\\
&\add{=(G_H-G_L)+(-U_H)-(-U_L)}\\
&\add{=(U_L-U_H)+(G_H-G_L).}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
\add{This proves the cost-bridge identity. By definition, logged sales shift the comparison toward $L$ exactly when $\Delta_Y^{\mathrm{CL}}>\Delta_D^{\mathrm{CL}}$, which is equivalent to $W^{\mathrm{CL}}>0$. If $\Delta_D^{\mathrm{CL}}<0$, reversal in favor of $L$ means $\Delta_Y^{\mathrm{CL}}>0$. Since $\Delta_Y^{\mathrm{CL}}=\Delta_D^{\mathrm{CL}}+W^{\mathrm{CL}}$, this occurs exactly when $W^{\mathrm{CL}}> -\Delta_D^{\mathrm{CL}}$; equality gives a tie. \qed}
\endproof

\add{The proof requires neither target ordering nor a sign restriction on $W^{\mathrm{CL}}$. Unlike the static setting, changing the evaluated demand path can change each policy's entire inventory and order-pipeline trajectory. Proposition~2 is therefore an exact, directly computable condition rather than a monotonicity result. It applies to absolute cost differences; relative percentage effects with changing denominators do not satisfy the same additive identity.}
